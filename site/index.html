Summary

Your broker is closed 135 hours a week. Solana isn't.

The gap is where retail risk actually lives.

Earnings leak on a Friday evening. A supplier catches fire on a Saturday. You watch it happen, you understand what it means for the things you own, and you can do nothing until 09:30 — at which point the stock doesn't open where it closed. It opens at a price that has already absorbed all of it.

Institutions hedge this with futures and swaps. For someone holding 100 shares of NVDA that door is shut: one ES contract is roughly $300k of notional, weekend puts cost a full weekend of time value, and extended-hours trading doesn't cover weekends at all.

Does that tape actually predict the open?

This is the load-bearing claim, so it's measured rather than asserted. Every close-to-open window compares how far the xStock moved while the market was shut against how far the stock actually gapped. The baseline is assuming no gap at all — precisely what you're stuck with today.

The hedge
Mechanism

A spot sell can't hedge shares held at a broker.

If you don't already hold the token, selling it doesn't reduce your exposure — you've just spent money. A hedge has to gain when the stock falls, which means borrowing the asset and selling it. The hedge leg composes two protocols that already exist: Kamino for the borrow, Jupiter for the execution.

  1. You enter what you actually hold

    A ticker and a share count. No brokerage API, no account linking, no credentials — nothing of yours is connected to anything.

  2. It measures the drift while you're locked out

    The matching xStock's move from the last real close, converted to dollars across your positions, with one alert per ticker per closed session — not forty as a threshold oscillates.

  3. Borrow the xStock, sell it

    Collateral is sized for Kamino's borrow factor — 1.66× on SPYx and QQQx, 2.25× on NVDAx and TSLAx — so a $50 short posts $207.50 of USDC. Anything that would open above 60% LTV is refused.

  4. After the open, it marks its own homework

    Actual gap, what your frozen position moved, how many percentage points the prediction was off by, and what the hedge earned at the official open.

Limits

What it doesn't do.

  • The signal is directional, not a magnitude. Sizing a hedge off the raw predicted move would over-hedge.
  • Holdings are manual entry. A ticker and a share count — there's no brokerage integration.
  • A short's legs aren't atomic. Each failure path leaves you flat rather than half-hedged, and says how to finish it.
  • Kamino's weekend oracle can stall on a large move, exactly when a hedge is most wanted. Gap Guard explains that rather than surfacing a raw program error.
  • Only SPYx, QQQx, NVDAx and TSLAx are shortable today. Everything else is collateral-only.
Run it

No account, no key, no money.

Dry-run mode uses live xStock and stock prices and simulates hedges, so the whole thing can be tried without connecting or funding anything. 

# dashboard on http://127.0.0.1:8788
npm install && cp .env.example .env
npm run dev

# score the core claim against real historical gaps
npm run backtest

Built for the Stocklana hackathon. 38 unit tests, and the security model is written up in the README — the dashboard holds a hot wallet, so it's loopback-only behind Host, Origin and custom-header checks that close CSRF and DNS rebinding.
